---
title: Forking lets divergent versions of a page coexist instead of forcing a merge
summary: Federated Wiki shares knowledge by copying pages between sites, so two people can keep different versions of the same page.
---

In [Federated Wiki](../../entities/federated-wiki/), editing someone else's page copies it onto the editor's own site; the original author can copy the revisions back or keep writing on the original. Every page carries a journal of the actions that changed it, so each copy keeps its history ([Cunningham, 2012](../../sources/on-federating-wiki/)).

**Inference:** sharing a research with a colleague can work the same way. The colleague forks the repository and continues it; nothing forces the two versions back into one. Whether the forks later meet is a choice made in a [view over both](../composing-wikis-as-a-view-makes-merges-reversible/), not a condition of sharing. What the colleague needs to make sense of the fork is covered in [sharing a graph needs added context](../a-graph-shared-beyond-its-authors-needs-added-context/).
