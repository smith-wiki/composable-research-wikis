---
title: Isolating researches gives up the gains of knowledge compiled once and shared across tasks
summary: Shared, versioned knowledge assets help most on questions that need several sources at once, which is what isolated researches cannot reuse.
---

ScholarStack compiles a collection of papers once into reusable, versioned assets that keep their provenance, at the level of single papers, of a domain, and of syntheses across papers. Agents that use the assets instead of re-reading papers for each task gain most on tasks that need evidence from several papers, and spend fewer tokens at query time on every task measured ([ScholarSeed AI Team, 2026](../../sources/scholarstack/)).

**Contradiction:** this cuts against [one topic per wiki](../one-topic-per-wiki-is-established-practice/). A research that starts from zero reads again what an older research already read, and cannot use its syntheses unless it cites them. **Inference:** citing [a pinned published version](../depending-on-a-pinned-version-decouples-researches/) recovers part of the gain without shared mutable state, because the older research's notes are versioned and keep their provenance, the same properties ScholarStack's assets have. **Uncertainty:** only the abstract was read; how much of the gain comes from sharing rather than from compilation itself is not known here.
