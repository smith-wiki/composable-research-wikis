## Overview

This captured arXiv abstract page presents ScholarStack, a layered framework for turning a paper collection into research assets that scientific agents can reuse across literature tasks. The authors address builders and researchers working on retrieval, question answering, evidence-grounded generation, and claim assessment. They position paper-level statements, domain organization, and cross-paper synthesis as shared infrastructure with versioning and provenance. The capture contains only the abstract page, not the paper's full text, so it supports the authors' stated design and reported headline results but does not permit inspection of methods, measurements, or limitations. For this research, it complements personal-wiki sources by showing how accumulated source interpretation can be organized for reuse instead of repeated task-by-task.

## Key points

- Task-specific scientific agents repeatedly retrieve, segment, and interpret the same papers, while understanding from one task is difficult to reuse in another. (Abstract)
- ScholarStack compiles a paper collection into reusable, versioned, provenance-preserving assets. (Abstract)
- Its three layers are source-grounded paper statements, domain-level organization, and evidence-grounded cross-paper syntheses. (Abstract)
- The reported quality improvements are concentrated in tasks that require evidence across papers, including multi-paper question answering and literature review generation. (Abstract)
- Query-time token cost fell for every task on which the authors measured it because assets were compiled once and reused. (Abstract)
