## Overview

This 2024 Squishy Computer newsletter article is Gordon Brander's first-party account of why Subconscious ended active operations. Addressed to beta testers and followers of the project, it records the team's outcome, reflects on the market window for decentralized social protocols, and argues that personal AI displaced decentralized notes as the more promising route to amplified intelligence. It is authoritative about the company's decision, the product's stated goal, and the export path offered to users, but its causal analysis of timing and product-market fit is the founder's retrospective rather than independent evaluation. Read beside the earlier Noosphere announcement, it shows both the protocol the team built and the product and sustainability limits that followed.

## Key points

- Subconscious announced that it would wind down active operations. ("Subconscious will be winding down active operations")
- The team built Noosphere as a multiplayer graph protocol for notes. ("a multiplayer graph protocol for notes")
- Brander says technical integration problems, a missed market window, and failure to find product-market fit prevented the project from succeeding. (Missing a window)
- The original product hypothesis combined a Roam-like tool for thought with a Twitter-like social network over a decentralized protocol. (What I think we got right)
- Brander concluded that personal AI, rather than decentralized notes, had become the stronger route to amplifying intelligence. (Aiming at the wrong target)
- Beta users could export their notes as plain text through the iOS file system and the Noosphere protocol after the app's TestFlight expired. (To our beta testers)
